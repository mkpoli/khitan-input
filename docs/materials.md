Otake's fonts, `KhitanSmall` and `KSS-Gengokenkyu`, identify glyphs during extraction and are never
written out. Font revisions between the papers changed some outlines; `data/otake_papers/` records how each
such glyph was identified, and five stay unidentified.

大竹昌巳 (2018) 「日本契丹学小史」 gives the history of the field and no data. 大竹昌巳 (2013) is the
predecessor of the 2015 paper; its text layer is OCR and has lost the Khitan.

`data/seal/` records the two seal-script covers of the Qingling 哀册: 36 blocks on the Daozong cover
and 16 on the Xuanyi cover. Wang 1931 identifies the inscriptions and reads the first line of each
epitaph text. The catalog records probable Unicode correspondences by shape. See [`data/seal/README.md`](../data/seal/README.md).

`data/inscriptions/inscriptions.tsv` is read from the two Wikipedia lists by `scripts/extract_wikipedia_inscriptions.py`. The rows are joined by position; the Chinese list has no row for the second fish-shaped tally. The two lists disagree in places: the first tally has one line of Khitan in English and two in Chinese, and the Langjun xingji fragment was found in 1980 in English and 1982 in Chinese. Neither list was checked against the works it cites.

The large-script seals are cut in a folded style after the Chinese nine-fold seal script, according to the English list's lead. [West's survey of Khitan seals](https://www.babelstone.co.uk/Blog/2012/10/khitan-seals.html) (2012) was found through a search and not opened; no seal face is transcribed here.

大竹昌巳 (2015c) locates 10 or more Chinese classical works behind quotations in Khitan inscriptions and adds
白居易「祭微之文」, 『後漢書』江革伝 and 『孟子』告子上. The dissertation cites it for mɵɵr (TAY 26) and
yaw- (YER 17); its 31 quotations are not transcribed here.

## The Jurchen comparison

Nine characters of the comparison page come from Kiyose's figure. Each Khitan form was drawn from
BabelStone's Private Use font and matched to the figure by shape; the match is this site's work, and
every pair was checked side by side. The codes are BabelStone's order, not a stable encoding:

| Gloss | Khitan | Jurchen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 天 | U+E000 | U+18E16 |
| 年 | U+E018 | U+19012 |
| 日 | U+E0C9 | U+18F7A |
| 月 | U+E0C6 | U+190FA |
| 龍 | U+E0FF | U+18F65 |
| 人 | U+E00A | U+1906F |
| 聽 | U+E47C | U+1907C |
| 中 | U+E345 | U+190FB |
| 福 | U+E034 | U+19182 |

Liu and Wang 2004 gloss U+E00A as 人 and U+E018 as 年, which agrees with the matches. The four
directions are not among the nine; Kiyose gives them only in Jurchen, and the sources place them with
Chinese rather than Khitan. Jurchen readings follow Jin Qizong's dictionary (1984).

## How the script is encoded

Unicode encodes graphemes, not blocks. Characters run left to right, then top to bottom within a
block. U+16FE4 KHITAN SMALL SCRIPT FILLER selects the layout with one grapheme on the first line
([Core Spec ch.18](https://www.unicode.org/versions/latest/core-spec/chapter-18/),
[N5002](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2018/18213-n5002-khitan-small-ad-hoc.pdf); the filler's exact
effect is read from these and Wikipedia). NamesList marks U+18C3E and U+18CD6–18CDA as tentatively
Jurchen Small Script; `character.jurchen_tentative` carries that. Noto Serif Khitan Small Script forms these blocks in its own shaping. U+18CFF is the blank.

## Found, not used yet

- [BabelStone Khitan Small](https://www.babelstone.co.uk/Fonts/KhitanSmall.html) v16.000, 2024: covers U+18CFF. Free for use, with no explicit redistribution grant. Not downloaded.
- [N4631](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2014/14234-n4631-khitan.pdf), China, 2014: 2,218 large-script characters with a document survey. PDF only; its tables are not parsed.
- [N5319](https://www.unicode.org/wg2/docs/n5319-KhitanLargeScriptEncoding.pdf), 2025: replaces N4631. [N5323](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2025/25166-wg2-n5323.pdf) (West, Zaytsev) criticises it as a glyph encoding without source references. Repertoire not checked.
- [吉本智慧子 (Aisin Gioro) 2012](http://www.ritsumei.ac.jp/acd/cg/lt/rb/627/627PDF/yosimoto.pdf), 立命館文學 627: reading notes, and who first proposed each value. Cited by N4725R; not opened. The largest missing layer.
- 大竹昌巳 (2025) TABLE IV–XIII: about 100 cognates with glyphs, in a scan. Not yet transcribed.
- [ja.wiktionary per-character pages](https://ja.wiktionary.org/wiki/%F0%98%AC%AD): copies of N4725R Table 6. [en.wiktionary Module:zkt-translit/data](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Module:zkt-translit/data): source not stated. Neither adds evidence.
- [Xebec33 Rime schemas](https://github.com/Xebec33/khitan_small_script_wubi), GPL-3.0: one author's Wubi codes, in the Private Use Area and built around CCAMC fonts. A design reference at most.
- CCAMC: not used, by this site's policy.
- [Brett-Khitan-Script-Decoder](https://github.com/nbbulk-dotcom/Brett-Khitan-Script-Decoder): excluded. Its decoding claim has no support.
- 康鹏 (2016), 「《马卫集书》中的契丹语词“Sh.rghūr（汉人）”」, 《西域研究》2016(3): 30–37: Khitan words in Arabic transcription (*ǰiaukūr, *suŋkur, *kur/*kui); the Khitan-script forms are images in the PDF, so no reading is added.
- 康鹏 (2017), 「马卫集书中的契丹“都城”」, 《民族研究》2017(2): *ūdkīn and *ūam in the same source, with the route east. Offprint PDF; the forms are images.
- 孙伯君 (2015), 「契丹小字碑铭中的金代年号」, 《满语研究》2015(1): 54–57: the Jin era names in the three Jin-dated small-script epitaphs, read through Jurchen. The glyphs are images in the PDF and are not transcribed here.
